Nutrition Targets

  • Starting calories: approximately 2,200–2,300 kcal/day.
  • Protein: approximately 140–160 g/day.
  • Water: about 2.5–3 L/day, with more if sweating heavily.
  • Fruit + vegetables: aim for at least 400 g/day combined.
  • Tea: 1–2 cups/day is fine. Jaggery is allowed in small amounts (about ½–1 tsp/cup); count it as added/free sugar.
  • Rice and roti do not need to be eliminated—control portions.
  • Prefer fish, eggs, dal, curd, paneer and moderate mutton; chicken is optional.

Late-Night Training Notes

  • Your 9:30–11:00 PM workout is acceptable if this schedule is consistent for you.
  • Keep the last 5–10 minutes of cardio easy and include a short cool-down.
  • Have a light protein-containing meal after training rather than a large oily dinner.
  • If hard cardio makes it difficult to sleep, reduce intensity and finish with easy walking/stretching.
  • Do not force 7,000 steps. Your current 1–2 km comfortable walking distance is enough to start; gym cardio such as cycling or elliptical still counts as useful exercise even if it does not add many steps.

Walking & Steps

Do not force 7,000 steps right now. Your current comfortable walking distance is about 1–2 km.

  • Week 1: 1–2 km/day as comfortable.
  • Week 2: 1.5–2 km/day.
  • Week 3: 2–2.5 km/day if comfortable.
  • Week 4+: gradually increase toward 3 km and later 7,000 steps if your body tolerates it.
  • Treadmill walking contributes to daily steps. Cycling/elliptical are useful cardio even though they do not add many steps.
  • You may split walking into 10–15 minute blocks.

Weight Tracking

Weigh in each morning after using the toilet and before eating/drinking, using the same scale and similar conditions. Use the 7-day average rather than reacting to a single reading. A temporary increase after starting gym can be water retention from exercise and does not automatically mean fat gain.

Safety & Recovery

  • Use weights that allow good technique and leave roughly 2–3 repetitions in reserve.
  • Rest about 60–90 seconds between most strength sets.
  • Saturday and Sunday are recovery-focused; daily gym attendance does not mean hard training every day.
  • Stop exercise and seek medical attention for chest pain, fainting, severe unusual breathlessness, or other concerning symptoms.
  • If walking is limited by persistent pain, significant breathlessness, dizziness or another symptom, get medical advice rather than pushing through it.

Key principle: Consistency for 6–8 weeks matters more than making every workout extreme.
